The Boardgame

PASTRY PIRATES

Rules of the Sugar Seas  ·  2–4 captains  ·  about an hour

For the wooden “Round Table” set. Bring your own gold coins — and one coin to flip.

The goal. You came to Tortuga to open a bakery — but your pantry is empty. Choose a secret recipe of five ingredients, sail out and gather them by docking, trading, and plundering — then be the first captain home to Tortuga to fire the ovens and be crowned Best Baker in the Caribbean .

Build the board

1
Lock the board. The four sea quadrants interlock — any quadrant fits any corner. Drop the Tortuga tile into the centre hole last.
2
Place the islands. Put the 7 island tiles in the bag. Going around the table, each captain draws one and places it, until all seven are down. Every island must keep: one full square of open water between itself and the outer wind ring, two squares clear of Tortuga, and a sailing lane (a square or two of water) from its neighbours.
3
Name the islands. Shuffle the 7 marker discs face-down and set one on each island, then flip them — each island grows that ingredient for this voyage.
4
Stock the shelves. On each island, place crates of its ingredient: one fewer than the number of captains — 3 crates each at 4 captains, 2 at 3, 1 at 2. Spare crates stay in the bank.
5
Set the docks. Butt one dock against each island’s shore and drop the mooring post through to lock it. A dock sits on open water — keep it off the wind ring and clear of Tortuga’s own four berths.
6
Drop the whirlpools. Each captain places one whirlpool tile on any square of the outer ring — one tile per captain.
Why the spacing rules? Ships sail around islands, never over them. A lane of water between everything keeps every dock reachable in every wind.

Set sail

7
Draw your recipe. Shuffle the 21 recipe maps and deal two to each captain. Keep one — that is your secret recipe of five ingredients — and return the other to the box unseen. Your recipe stays hidden all game; the crates in your hold are public.
8
Draw lots for sailing order. The captain who most recently ate a pastry spins the first-player wheel; whoever it points to sails first, then play goes clockwise. This order never changes.
9
Take your coins — patience pays. The first captain takes 3 , and each later captain takes one more than the captain before them: 4, then 5, then 6. Going first is worth exactly that much.
10
Moor the ships. Every ship starts in one of Tortuga’s four berths.
11
Wake the weather. Spin the compass’s NOW needle — that is day one’s wind — then set tomorrow’s sky (page 3, one forecast spin and one storm spin). You’re ready. First captain, sail!

Wind & weather — the start of every day

A day is one full lap of the table. Each morning, before anyone sails, the day’s first captain tends the compass:

1
Set today’s wind. Is the storm cloud sitting on the compass? Then a storm breaks — see below. Otherwise, turn the NOW needle to where the forecast arrow points. The forecast is never wrong.
2
Spin the forecast arrow. That is tomorrow’s wind, committed now, for all to see and plan around.
3
Spin the storm spinner. If it lands on the storm sector, set the cloud on top of the forecast arrow: a storm arrives tomorrow, and nobody knows which way it will blow. If the last two days both stormed, skip this spin — the sky is spent.

Sailing the wind

Sailing is free. Move up to 4 squares, one at a time, north, south, east or west, mixing directions as you please. But if even one step of your route goes directly against the NOW needle, your whole move that turn is capped at 2 squares. Across the wind costs nothing extra.

  • You may sail past other ships, but never end on one.
  • Islands and Tortuga are land — sail around, never over.
  • Only one ship fits a berth. An occupied berth is closed to you.
  • You may always stay put.

When the storm breaks

Spin the NOW needle itself — that is the storm’s direction. Every ship is blown 3 squares downwind before anyone acts, starting with the ship furthest downwind. Land and other ships stop you short — that’s the whole rule. Nobody loses a turn; a dock is just water the storm can push you onto or off. Then take the cloud off and set tomorrow’s sky as usual (steps 2 and 3).

Anchoring with land at your back so the storm cannot move you is not cowardice — it is seamanship.

The trade winds & whirlpools

The outer ring of sea flows in one mighty clockwise current. Enter any ring square — by sail or by storm — and you are swept clockwise to the next whirlpool, where your movement ends. A daring shortcut for a captain in a hurry, and a hazard for one who isn’t.

Dock — treasure, wages, and the market

Sitting in an island’s berth, flip a coin: heads you strike buried treasure and take 3 from the bank; tails you spend the turn working the docks for 1 . Either way, you may then buy one crate from that island — with the coins you just earned, if you like.

Prices climb as shelves empty. The first crate an island sells costs 3 ; each later crate costs one more than the last — 3, then 4, then 5. Get there first, or pay for dawdling. You may buy crates that are not on your recipe: they make excellent trade bait.

The black market. When an island’s shelf is bare, the bank will sell one last crate of that ingredient for a flat 10 — and you must still sail to that island’s berth to collect it. Dear, but no recipe is ever dead.

You may stay moored and flip again next turn, as long as you please.

Trade — hail the whole table

Announce what you want and what you offer — crates, coins, or both. Every captain holding what you asked for answers, in sailing order: accept, refuse, or name their own price. Hear every answer, then strike one deal or walk away. One round of haggling only — no countering a counter. Anyone may trade any crates; cargo is public, so the table always knows who holds what.

Attack — one broadside each

Fight a ship on an orthogonally adjacent square. You cannot attack an empty hold — there is nothing to take. Pay 2 to the bank for powder, then attacker and defender each flip a coin at once:

The flipsWhat happens
vs Heads beats tails — the heads ship wins.
+ If one ship lies downwind of the other — on the side the NOW needle points toward — the downwind ship wins. Crosswind, the cannonballs collide: no result yet.
+ The defender may flee, free: sail away at once under the normal sailing rules, and the battle ends with nothing gained. Or they stand: no result yet.

No result? The attacker may pay 2 for a fresh broadside and flip alone — heads and it lands, tails and they may pay again, as often as they can afford. Break off instead, and the battle ends with nobody gaining a thing (the powder stays spent).

The spoils: the winner takes one crate of their choosing from the loser’s hold. Nobody changes squares.

Calling the fight — the side bet. Before the first coins fly, every captain not in the battle may call the winner aloud, free. A correct call pays 2 from the bank when the battle ends. A battle with no winner pays no caller.
Wind wins ties. Downwind means the side the NOW needle points toward: if the wind blows east, the easternmost of the two ships is downwind — the wind is at the other’s back, spoiling their aim. Pick your moment, and your square, before you pick your fight.
Pass. Any turn may simply end: take 1 from the bank, lean on the rail, and tell the table what strange creature you saw in the water.

Raids — no berth is safe

Every dock is raidable. Attacking a moored ship follows the battle rules exactly — a berth protects nobody, not even a captain who has already fired up the ovens. Steal a crate a finished captain’s recipe needs, and their bakery goes cold: they are back in the game, and must sail out and replace what was taken. Steal a spare crate, and their bake stands.

Winning — first baker home

When you are moored at Tortuga holding every ingredient on your recipe map, declare “Start your bakery!” as your action. Then every other captain gets exactly one last turn, continuing around the table from your seat — one final chance to reach home with a full recipe, or to raid yours.

Then the ovens are lit:

  • One captain home with a full recipe — they are the Best Baker in the Caribbean.
  • Several captains home — there is no contest: they bake together, and Best Baker goes to whoever brought the most to the bench — most crates in the hold (all crates, recipe or not), then most coins, then whoever got home first.
  • Nobody home (the raid landed) — the voyage simply continues, day after day, until somebody is.

The finer points

  • Recipes are secret; everything else is public. Coins, cargo, prices, the wind and its forecast — all open. Watch what a rival buys and asks for, and you’ll guess their recipe soon enough.
  • Coins come from docking (3 or 1), correct battle calls (+2), passing (+1), trades, and the staggered start. The bank never runs dry.
  • Crates come from buying at docks, winning battles, and trades — never from the flip alone.
  • The storm and the ring: blown into the trade winds, you are swept to the next whirlpool as usual — the sweep ends that ship’s storm push.
  • Storms never strike three days running. After two in a row, skip the storm spin.
  • An occupied berth cannot be entered — by sail or by storm; you stop short. Evict nobody: fights take cargo, not squares.
  • Hemmed in? If a route rule ever leaves you unable to move, you may always stay put — and always Pass.
  • Two captains: each island stocks a single crate (it costs 3), you place two whirlpools in total, and coins start 3 and 4. Expect a knife-fight for the black market.
  • The app is the tie-breaker. If a situation is not covered here, play it as playpastrypirates.com plays it — the boardgame and the app are one game.

Optional expansion — the Ovens Bake-off

A memory duel at the finish line, straight from the app. Agree to it before the voyage starts; it replaces “Winning” on page 5.

  • Reaching Tortuga with a full recipe no longer wins — it lights your ovens. The voyage sails on around you, and you may still be raided.
  • At the end of each day, every captain at the ovens attempts the bake. Lay your five recipe crates in a row in your recipe map’s order while all watch. The captain to your left turns them face-down and makes three slow swaps, one pair at a time, in plain view.
  • Now name them back: point to the crates in recipe order, flipping each as you call it. A correct call stays face-up — and stays solved for every later attempt. Your first miss ends the attempt (turn it back over).
  • All five face-up — you baked it! The voyage ends with that day; if two captains bake the same day, Best Baker (page 5) settles it. Otherwise: tomorrow evening the hider re-hides only your unsolved crates, three fresh swaps, and you try again.
  • Pay 1 before guessing to have the swaps shown again, slowly — as many times as you can afford. Gold’s last, best use.

A day of Pastry Pirates — quick reference

  1. Weather: cloud up? Storm — spin NOW, blow every ship 3 downwind, remove cloud. Else NOW ← forecast. Then spin forecast; spin storm spinner (cloud over the forecast arrow on a storm).
  2. Each captain, in order: Sail (up to 4; any upwind step caps it at 2), then one act: Dock · Trade · Attack · Start your bakery · Pass.
  3. First bakery declared: everyone else gets one last turn — then crown the Best Baker.
