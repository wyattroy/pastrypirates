The voyage ahead

Twenty questions about where Pastry Pirates sails after launch: money, accounts, ways to play, merch and the real-world board. Each one is built from what comparable games actually do.

We'll go one at a time in chat and you answer out loud. If you'd rather dictate a batch, start each answer with its number. Every "others" note links to the page it came from; anything that couldn't be verified was left out.

Already settled, so not asked again

  • It's "a living game I grow for years" (your ruling, 30 Aug).
  • Logins and paid digital merch come within a month of launch, so the questions ask what they're for, not whether.
  • Today: free, no login, no download, no ads, 1 to 4 captains, solo, pass-the-wheel or online crew, and a 🍪 Buy me a cookie button.
  • Bots and humans always play by identical rules.

The shape of it

1–2
1

What does "a living game I grow for years" look like when it's working?

Pick your scoreboard: players a week, money, hours you get back, letters from families? If thousands played and it never earned a cent, would that count as winning?

What others did
  • Wordle had fewer than 100 players at first, then 2 million within weeks. It never charged anything. The New York Times bought it for "low seven figures". CNBC
  • Lichess is free forever to millions, and donations pay for its servers and one full-time developer. lichess.org
2

Hobby, side business, or company?

What monthly amount would change something for you: covering the Firebase bill, a few hundred dollars, or a salary? And how many hours a week of tax, support and payments are you willing to own?

What it takes
  • Paddle becomes the legal seller and files sales tax worldwide, for 5% + 50¢ per checkout. Paddle
  • Stripe is cheaper per sale, but you stay the seller. Its full tax-filing service starts at $90 a month. Stripe
  • EU tax on digital sales applies even to a US seller. EU

Money

3–8
3

What will Pastry Pirates promise never to do?

Your About page already says "no cookies, no ads, nothing sold to anyone." Is that a launch-day fact or a forever promise?

Which of these go on the never list: ads, paying to win, selling data, mystery-box purchases, charging to play with friends?

What others do
  • Lichess promises "no ads, no subscriptions", free forever. Ticket to Ride advertises "ad-free". Lichess Marmalade
  • Research consistently finds that players accept paying for looks and resent paying for power. Springer
  • On phones, Gartic Phone players complain that ads lag the game. App Store
4

When a crew sails together, should one captain's purchase carry the whole table?

Does "one captain pays, the whole crew gets the extras" feel generous and right, or does it give too much away?

What others do
  • Jackbox: "only one person in your group needs to own" the game. Jackbox
  • Colonist: "to play with your friends only 1 player needs a Membership." Colonist
  • Board Game Arena: one premium player at the table unlocks a premium game. BGA forum
5

What could someone buy that never helps them win?

Which are Pastry Pirates: new seas or maps, new captains, ship and flag looks, new recipes, a winter or night sea, sound and music packs? And what is sacred and stays free for everyone?

What others sell
  • Wingspan sells $4.99 look-only packs next to $9.99–$11.99 expansions. Steam
  • Ticket to Ride sells new maps from $3.99 to $34.99. Steam
  • Colonist sells colours, avatars and extra maps. Catan Universe sells avatar outfits and pets. Colonist
6

One price, a membership, or a supporter's badge?

Should the 🍪 Buy me a cookie button grow into something: a flag on your ship, your name in the credits, a membership with perks, or a one-time "full game" unlock?

What others do
  • Ticket to Ride costs $14.99 once, with expansions sold on top. Steam
  • Board Game Arena Premium is 36 a year. Their own announcement says some members use none of the perks and treat it as a donation. BGA
  • Lichess supporters get "Patron wings" on their profile, and nothing is ever locked. Lichess
7

Does the game live only at playpastrypirates.com?

Or can you picture your players finding it on a game portal, on Steam, in the App Store, or inside a Discord call?

The trade-offs
  • Poki splits ad money 50/50 on the players it sends you, but asks for 5 years of web exclusivity. Poki
  • CrazyGames has no exclusivity and shows ads, but you pay for multiplayer servers yourself. CrazyGames
  • Steam costs $100 per game. The App Store costs $99 a year, takes a 15% cut, and rejects apps that are just "a repackaged website". Apple
  • Colonist runs inside Discord as a game friends play during a voice call. Colonist
8

Is Pastry Pirates yours for keeps?

If the right offer came, would you license it to a publisher or sell it outright? What's untouchable: the name, the art, the rules, the voice?

What others did
  • New board-game designers usually earn about 5% royalties, with $500–$2,000 up front. Stonemaier pays $10,000 up front, and the publisher decides what changes. Stonemaier
  • Exploding Kittens has a version inside Netflix Games. Netflix

Accounts

9–11
9

When a captain signs in, what do they get to keep?

A win count? A log of past voyages? Captains or looks they've unlocked? Their purchases on any device? A list of their crewmates?

What others do
  • Chess.com guests can play, but keep no game history. Chess.com
  • Among Us guests get preset chat and random names; an account unlocks typed chat and a chosen name. Innersloth
  • Games usually put ratings, history, custom names, saved progress and purchases behind a sign-in.
10

Must anyone ever sign in just to play?

Your About page promises "no login, no download."

Does that stay true forever for playing, with signing in only for extras? Or is there a line where it's worth asking?

What others do
  • Jackbox and Gartic Phone never ask anyone to sign in. Gartic
  • Lichess guests play, but only unrated games. Lichess
  • Firebase, which the game already uses, can quietly give every player a guest account. They can attach Google or email later and keep everything. Firebase
11

Who's at the table, and what can they say to each other?

A cute pastry game can look like a kids' game to a regulator. US children's privacy law covers games aimed at under-13s, and counts a screen name as personal information. UK law requires the most private settings by default for children.

Families with young kids, adults, classrooms? And chat: none, preset pirate phrases, emoji, or typed?

What others do
  • Among Us: under-13s need a parent's permission, and guests only get preset "Quick Chat". Innersloth
  • Jackbox ships a family-friendly filter, extended timers and subtitles. Jackbox
  • US rule in plain terms. FTC UK ICO

Ways to play

12

A Daily Voyage: the same sea for everyone, every day?

Does it fit? And when you finish, what does the share card show: days to home, your recipe, your broadsides won?

What others do
  • A player invented Wordle's spoiler-free emoji grid, the creator built it in, and press credits it for the game's spread. Wikipedia
  • Dominion gives one free daily puzzle. Chess.com gives three. Steam
  • The game already builds the same board every time from the same seed, so this is the cheapest mode on the list.
13

Friends only, or strangers too?

Should a captain be able to press "find me a crew" and sail with strangers? Is there a ranked ladder with seasons? Or is Pastry Pirates a friends-and-family game?

What others do
  • Colonist runs ranked seasons, and the top 50 win free membership. Colonist
  • Jackbox has no matchmaking at all: you bring your own friends and your own voice call. Jackbox
  • Ranked play needs enough people online at once, and it brings cheating reports.
14

A slow voyage: one move a day, with a nudge when it's your turn?

Would your players sail one voyage across a week, from their phones, in between everything else?

What others do
  • Board Game Arena runs games at 1–2 moves a day with email reminders, and says over 98% of them finish. BGA
  • Since iOS 16.4, a web game saved to an iPhone home screen can send notifications. WebKit
