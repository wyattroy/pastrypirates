<objective>
Make the trade-wind rim readable before a player commits to it: any rim square in their move
highlights gets an obviously different treatment, and at the same moment a faded ghost of their own
boat marks where the ride ends. One tap still commits — nothing is added to the interaction, only to
what is visible while the choice is open.

Purpose: WIND-05 is the item players get blindsided by. The fix has to work on a phone, where there
is no hover, and it has to work for a guest, whose board renders through a different function from
the host's. Both constraints point at the same answer: the change belongs inside the two shared
builders, so the host and the guest cannot diverge — which is the second application of a precedent
this codebase extracted precisely because those two paths had already forked once.

Output: a rim-aware `sailHighlightRect`, a new `sailGhostBoats` called once by each transport, and a
parity assertion that both keep calling both.
</objective>

**`sailGhostBoats(cells, seat, cellPx, svg)` (D-13, D-15).** Add and export a new function beside `sailHighlightRect`. For each cell in `cells`, skip it unless it is in the engine's rim set; look its landing square up in `appState.game.rimHead`; skip it if that lookup is empty; de-duplicate by the landing square's key so that two rim cells belonging to the same arc produce one ghost and two rim cells on different arcs produce two — D-13's two-ghost case then falls out of the de-duplication rather than needing its own branch. D-25's rule that animated layers must be HTML rather than SVG children is about elements that animate every frame; a ghost boat never animates, and the real ship images are SVG for the same reason.

Do not compute the sweep path. `rimSweepPath`, `rimSweepCurve`, `rimSweepPointAt` and `rimSweepDurationMs` exist and animate the real boat along the rim, and none of them is needed here — the landing square is a `rimHead` lookup and nothing more (D-15).
