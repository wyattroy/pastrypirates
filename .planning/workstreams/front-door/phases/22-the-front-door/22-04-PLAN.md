## Why this plan is not autonomous

D-11 is one of the phase's two human approval gates: Claude captures candidates, **Wyatt picks the
one that ships**. Task 2 is a blocking `checkpoint:decision`. Everything either side of it is
unattended. Wyatt's choice cannot be inferred or auto-approved — picking the frame that best sells
the game is a judgement about his own game.

## Precondition this plan depends on

`docs/DRIVING-THE-GAME.md`'s solo-start recipe **must already drive the name modal** rather than the
removed welcome-screen field. Plan 01 Task 1 fixes it. If that fix has not landed, the capture
session will stall on the welcome screen at the very first step — which is the exact symptom
`22-RESEARCH.md` Pitfall 3 predicts and the reason the doc fix was scheduled into the modal-creating
task rather than left as a follow-up.

  <action>
Play a real solo game in a browser and capture several candidate frames of a busy mid-game board.

**A. Set up per the project's own procedure.** Serve on a port not used earlier in this session
(`python3 -m http.server <port>`) — both Safari and Chrome cache ES modules per URL, and reusing a
port has produced phantom bugs in this project at least three times. Run `localStorage.clear()` and
reload. Rename the browser tab being driven to make it obvious it is under automation, and rename it
back when finished.

**B. Start and play.** Use the section-3 solo-start recipe as Plan 01 rewrote it: click the Play Solo
card, set the modal's name input, click the modal's confirm control. Then drive turns using the
section 4/5d procedure — remembering that the coin wrapper is itself the flip control. Play far
enough that the board is genuinely busy: roughly six to twelve rounds, until ships are scattered
across the islands rather than clustered at the start, several island crates have been taken, and at
least two captains have ingredients in their holds. Do not arrange or fake any of this — play through
it. Set the viewport wide enough that the wide desktop layout engages and the board renders at least
1200px across (1440x900 is a reasonable starting point).

**C. Capture four to six candidates** at different moments once the board qualifies, favouring frames
where the wind spinner is visible and no modal, overlay or transient narration banner is covering the
board. **Do not capture the end-of-voyage celebration or the bakeoff** — D-10 excludes it: it shows
the payoff, spoils it, and is not what a player spends an hour doing.

**D. Normalise and stage them.** For each capture, produce a JPEG 1200px wide and 663px tall (the
~1.81:1 ratio of the existing `og-image.jpg`, so the asset stays a direct swap candidate later),
using the macOS `sips` tool that is already available — resample to 1200 wide, then centre-crop to
663 tall, writing JPEG. Save them into a new `assets/about-candidates/` directory as
`candidate-1.jpg` through `candidate-N.jpg`. Commit them: the next task is a blocking checkpoint and
these files must survive a context reset.

**E. Write a one-line description of each candidate** — what the board is showing in it — into the
task output, so the checkpoint can present a real choice rather than a list of filenames.
  </action>
