## The headline

**Lowering the dock price makes purses WORSE, not better.** Your instinct was that cheaper crates
would leave captains with less spare change — the opposite happens: a cheaper crate means every
purchase eats less of a captain's purse, so more coin sits there unspent. The share of games where
somebody ends up holding over 10 coins jumps from about half (52%) today to about six in seven (85%)
under your proposed 1–3 dock range.

**Raising the battle cost to 4 barely moves purses, and prices a few more captains out of ever
affording a fight they wanted.** It does cut how often ships fight (down about a third), but the
coin it saves doesn't show up in anyone's purse, because a battle's prize was never coins — it's a
crate. Today, about 1 captain in 50 never once affords a fight or a crate they had reason to want,
across a whole voyage; at battle cost 4, that rises to about 1 in 19.

**Neither of your two levers, alone or together, gets purses under control.** The dock-price lever
moves the number in the wrong direction; the battle-cost lever barely moves it at all. Read on for
the numbers themselves and what they leave open.

## Against your target

Your target, quoted exactly: *"every player feels able to do what they want at least once per game,
and no player holds more than about 10 coins."* That's two separate questions, answered separately.

**"No player holds more than about 10 coins" — measured directly, no proxy needed.** The table's
"worst purse anyone held" column already answers it: every single setting, including today's,
blows past 10 by a wide margin (34–55 coins at the extreme). The share of *player-games* where
somebody crosses 10 coins at any point in the voyage:

| Setting | Share of games where a captain held over 10 coins |
|---|---|
| Today (baseline) | 52.3% |
| Your dock idea alone | 85.4% (**worse**) |
| Your battle idea alone | 51.1% (about the same) |
| Both of your ideas | 85.6% (**worse**) |

**"Every player feels able to do what they want at least once" — this one needed a proxy, and here
is the one chosen, so you can disagree with it.** There's no direct "did a captain feel able" signal
in the game — the closest measurable stand-in is: *on a captain's own turn, was there something in
reach worth doing (an adjacent rival worth robbing, or a dock selling a crate their recipe still
needs) that they could not afford?* Tallied per captain per whole voyage — did that captain have a
reason to act **and never once, the entire game, could afford it**:

| Setting | Share of player-games "boxed out" all game |
|---|---|
| Today (baseline) | 2.0% |
| Your dock idea alone | 0.8% (slightly better) |
| Your battle idea alone | 5.3% (**worse**) |
| Both of your ideas | 1.6% (about the same as today) |

Today's game already clears this bar reasonably well — captains are rarely fully boxed out. The
battle-cost idea is the one that hurts it, because 4 coins is a real bite out of a purse that
started with only 3.

## What the numbers do not tell you

- **This is bot play, not your table.** All four strategies are the shipping AI captains
  (`pirate`, `trader`, `balanced`, `rusher`), not a human weighing a purchase the way you or a
  friend would. A human captain who *chooses* to hoard coins would move these numbers in ways no
  bot here will.
- **"Worst purse anyone held" is a peak, not a typical outcome** — it is the single richest moment
  across 300 games × 4 seats × every turn of the voyage. The median (the "typical purse" column) is
  the more honest read of an ordinary captain's ordinary turn.
- **Neither candidate was tuned — only the two values you named were tried.** There may be a setting
  between today's and your candidates, or a different number entirely (`passCoin`, `dockHeads`,
  `dockTails`), that does what you actually want. This table answers "do your two ideas work,"
  not "what would."
- **The proxy for "felt able to act" is a judgement call, not a measurement of feeling.** It only
  catches two of the game's several actions (attacking, buying a needed crate) — not trading,
  fishing (v2 rule 3 already removed that), or simply sailing somewhere. A captain who never wanted
  to fight or buy in a given voyage reads as "never boxed out" by this proxy, correctly, but that
  captain also never got a chance to be blocked.

## The instrument, red-proofed before being believed

Before the four rows above were trusted, `scripts/economy_table.js` was checked against
`scripts/bot_ladder4.js` — the existing, already-trusted balance simulator — on the identical
construction (100 games, seed family ×7919, no overrides): both scripts reported **14.77 mean
rounds per voyage** and **0 unfinished voyages**, matching exactly. Only after that match did the
300-game matrix above get run.

The held-out seed family `scripts/bot_ladder4.js` reserves for close calls was not needed — no row
above sits close enough to another to need a second look. It stays unspent.
