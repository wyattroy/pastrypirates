# Phase 02.2 Plan 09 (Group G): the six the vision judge found

**One-liner:** four of the six judged faults were real and are fixed — the battle ceremony no longer
writes over the captains card, every crate circle carries its name, an ask pill can no longer sit
inside the captains box, and the recipe sheet has stopped wobbling hard enough to slice its own
subtitle — while the other two did not reproduce, and measuring one of them turned up a far worse
bug that made every prompt in the game invisible and untappable.

---

## What a player gets

**In a battle on a phone, you can read the coin's caption again.** "Crosswind — two heads and the
cannonballs collide" and "Tap the coin, captain — let fate decide" were being drawn on top of the
CAPTAINS card, with the captain names showing through the white text. The whole ceremony now sits in
the strip of board between the wind ribbon and the captains box.

**Every circle you can tap now says what it is.** Offering a crate in a trade used to bloom three
circles that were nothing but a picture — no words at all, and nothing for a screen reader either.
They now read "Toasty Wheat", "Fresh Milk", "Crystal Sugar", exactly as the question one screen
earlier already did.

**A long question no longer sits inside the captains box.** A three-line trade answer used to run
its last two lines over the top of the card and hide the word CAPTAINS entirely. Every ask now keeps
the same clear air above the card that everything else on the stage keeps.

**The recipe sheet has stopped shaking.** The two recipe cards were pulsing at 111% of their size on
a 1.15-second loop — big enough that they climbed 15px and covered the bottom two-thirds of "Tap a
recipe to highlight its docks" for half of every second, and big enough that the two cards collided
with each other by 34px at the peak. They sit still now; the chosen card still glows orange.

**And the one nobody asked about, which is the biggest of the six:** if you drag on the sea and lift
your finger anywhere off the board — over the captains box, past the edge of the screen — the game
used to go permanently deaf. Every prompt dropped to 13% opacity and stopped taking taps, with
nothing on screen to say why. It reads exactly like a frozen game, and the only way out was to tap
the sea again, which nobody would think to do. That cannot happen now.

**Size:** four of the six judged faults fixed, two reported as not reproducing with the measurement,
and one unlisted bug found and fixed. **It leaves undone:** nothing from this plan's list; the two
pre-existing gate failures at 820×1180 that Group F documented are still there and still not ours.

## Fault 5, in full — because the plan asked for the stacking measured, not guessed

**The judge's reading was that the narration bubble is drawn UNDERNEATH the sail squares. It is not,
and three independent measurements say so.**

**1. The stacking chain, walked to the root, on the live page.** Not the z-index numbers on their
own — every property that *creates* a stacking context, because a context is what the plan suspected:

```
.pp4Bub   position:absolute  z-index:26   ->  #pp4Fx      position:fixed  z-index:21
.sailCell position:absolute  z-index:auto ->  #sailHost   position:absolute z-index:2
                                          ->  #boardwrap  position:fixed  z-index:5
both ->  body.pp4Stage  (transform: matrix(1,0,0,1,0,0) — this IS the context they share)
```

`#pp4Fx` and `#boardwrap` are **siblings inside one stacking context**, at z-index **21 and 5**.
Nothing between them inverts it: no opacity below 1, no filter, no isolation, no blend mode, no
`will-change`, no `contain`, no perspective on either chain. The bubble wins, and it wins everywhere,
not by luck.

**2. The placement was not even on a square.** Posed with the ENGINE's own sail window
(`g.reachableFrom(p)`, drawn with the game's own `sailHighlightRect`), the bubble stood on **0 of 19**
squares — D-38's search found a clear spot. Red-proofed: the same bubble with no squares on the
board reports 0 overlaps too, so the probe was re-run with squares present and the count is a real
measurement rather than a check that could not fail.

**3. The bubble's computed opacity was 1**, background `rgb(255,253,242)`, fully opaque.

**So what was in the picture?** I measured the gate PNG itself. Inside the bubble's box, over the gap
of open water between two sail squares, the water is lightened from (75,146,170) to (105,167,184) —
solve for alpha against the bubble's own cream and all three channels agree at **α ≈ 0.17–0.20**. The
bubble was on top, at about 18%.

*(My first pass at that got it wrong and it is worth recording why: I compared a point inside the
bubble against a patch of water 80px away with different texture, concluded "no cream at all", and
nearly filed the opposite finding. The reading that stands compares the same water column crossing
the bubble's own top and bottom edges — HARD-WON-LESSONS §2, verify against an independent path.)*

**The only thing in the game that fades a bubble AND hides the whole prompt at once is
`body.pp4Peek`** — hold-the-sea, Wyatt's own sanctioned gesture. Confirmed by driving it: a finger
held on the sea takes the bubble to exactly **0.13** and `#pp4Prompt` to 0.13 with `pointer-events:none`;
a single ordinary tap takes it to **0.257** and back over about 500ms. That matches the gate frame,
and it also explains why solo-desktop-014 shows sail squares with no ask pill anywhere — the pill was
there, at 13%.

**Verdict: fault 5 does not reproduce. The words were ghosted by the gesture, caught mid-transition
by a screenshot.**

### …and then the thing that does reproduce

`pointerdown` on `#boardwrap` adds `pp4Peek` unconditionally. The only code that removes it was bound
to `#boardwrap` alone, with no pointer capture. **A pointer that goes down on the sea and comes up
anywhere else never disarms it.** Measured (`group_g_peek_probe.mjs`, case D): press on the sea, move
to the captains column, release — `body.pp4Peek` is still set **2.5 seconds later, unattended**, with
every prompt at 13% opacity and `pointer-events:none`. `ptrs` leaked the same way, taking the pinch
and pan state with it. The before/after screenshots are the clearest pair in this plan: in the first
the board is bright and there is no prompt anywhere, only a 13% ghost of "…k sets sail"; in the
second the sail prompt, the Stay put circle and the squares are all solid.

`setPointerCapture` — which the End of Voyage drag one screen over already uses — was tried and
rejected: capturing on `#boardwrap` retargets the compatibility mouse events too, and a sail square
answers a plain `click`, so it would have broken sailing to fix a fade. The handler is bound to the
**window** instead, and it is the same function rather than a second copy, so there is one answer to
"is a finger still on the sea". The guard (`if (!ptrs.has(e.pointerId)) return`) is what makes one
binding safe, and it also stops a tap that *began* off the board from reaching the double-tap zoom.

## Fault 6, in full — one gap rule, reached from both, and the judge was reading a pulse

The plan asked whether battle prompts reach the fan's placement at all. **They do.** `.btlBtn`
disqualifies a panel from the radial bloom outright (`menuButtons`), so the "Fire again / Break off"
circles in solo-desktop-020 cannot be `.btlBtn` — they are ordinary `.apBtn` from `ask()`, and they go
through exactly the same placement as every other fan.

Measured, the battle pair beside a plain two-option control at the same board square:

| | layout circle | centre-to-centre | gap (layout) | gap (painted) | transform |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| battle pair, mid-board | 66px | **83.00** | 17.0 | 14.9 | scale 1.032 |
| battle pair, cornered | 66px | **82.98** | 17.0 | 12.7 | scale 1.065 |
| plain control, cornered | 66px | **83.07** | 17.1 | 17.0 | scale 1.002 |

**The spacing is identical.** D = 66, GAP = round(66/4) = 17 — Group F's derived quarter, intact. The
whole variation is in the *painted* box, because D-32's `sailBounce` pulses every petal to scale 1.11
on staggered delays, and `getBoundingClientRect` returns the transformed box (BOARD-RENDERING §7:
"a size ratio is not a size"). At the peak, two 66px circles 83px apart show **83 − 71.9 = 11.1px** of
white between them — which is the judge's "about 10px apart", to the pixel.

**Verdict: fault 6 does not reproduce.** I did not touch the gap. Weakening or "fixing" a rule that
measures correct is how a rule stops meaning anything.

*The same reading disposes of the second half of the judge's phone note ("that circle and the
'- coins only -' circle sit about 10px apart"): measured off solo-phone-021, 67px cream disks 82.8px
apart — the same 83, the same rule.*

## What changed, fault by fault

### F1 — the ceremony is laid out in the board band (`cerBandTick`, stage.js)

The veil is `inset:0`, so its flex column centred in the whole 664px window while the captains card
starts at y433. The scrim still covers everything — dimming the ribbon and the card is its job — but
the **column** is now given only the band, by padding the veil's foot with the strip the card occupies
and its head with the ribbon's. `capBandBottom()` is the existing answer to "where does the captains
card start" (the plan's own instruction: do not invent a new number), and it already returns the full
viewport height in side-by-side — so `stacked` is false on desktop, the head padding is zero, and the
**desktop ceremony is byte-identical**, verified rect by rect against the pristine tree.

**Padding the foot alone was tried first and moved the fault rather than fixing it**: the column then
centred in 0..433 instead of 88..433, and the title landed at y39.1 against a ribbon that ends at y45
— 5.9px into it, and into the wind pill below that. Fixing one overlap by making another is not a fix
(rule 8), and it is recorded here rather than quietly corrected.

**And the column's own air closes up when the band is short**, because on a phone the ceremony is
genuinely taller than the band it has to live in: 22.4 + 216.1 + 19.4 + 19 = 276.9px of content, plus
three 26px gaps = 354.9px, in a band of 345px. Something has to give and it is the **air** — never the
words, never the coin, which is the thing being asked for. The gap is derived from the four children's
rendered heights, the band the renderer produced, and the 6px of clear air every other stacked floater
in this file already leaves; it saturates at the stylesheet's own resting value, **read** from a new
`--pp4CerGapMax` custom property rather than copied, so there is one 26 in the project and not two.
On the phone it lands at 18px; on desktop and on any tall band the arithmetic saturates and nothing
changes at all. Stable rather than oscillating, because no term in it depends on the gap.

### F2 — `crateOpt`'s short form carries the crate's name (flow.js)

`short` was `iconImg(...)` — the picture and nothing else, with `alt=""`. The radial fan prefers
`short`, so a captain's own crates bloomed as three unlabelled pictures while the WANT prompt one
screen earlier named all seven (solo-phone-011). Same gesture, two behaviours, which is rule 8.

It is `ilabelImg`'s own components — `iconImg` + `iname` — so the circle and the sentence name the
crate identically and there is no second spelling to keep in step; the `<br>` is the in-circle form
the trade's own answer circles already use (icon above, words below). **The count travels into the
short form too**: it is load-bearing here (the black market takes any two, the same crate twice
included), so dropping it in the one place a player actually taps would be losing information rather
than shortening it. Every crate name in the game is 13 characters or fewer, inside the 16 that
`menuButtons()` already treats as circle-sized — the fan's own existing rule, not a new number. The
`alt=""` is now correct rather than a gap: it is a decorative icon beside real text.

### F3 — the ask pill's foot clears the captains card (stage.js)

`capT - 44` is **a constant standing in for the pill's height**. 44 is one line of pill plus the 6px
of air, and it has been right only for one-line asks; a trade's answer runs to three lines and a
counter-offer to five. The clamp is applied once, to whichever spot the placement chose, rather than
to each of the four branches — and the branch that actually overran in the measurement (above the
boat, `sy - R - 96`) had **no bottom bound at all**. It is applied on *use* rather than on *store*, so
the pill lock keeps the pill still across a turn while a later, taller prompt in that same turn still
clears the card. Derived from the pill's own rendered height and the file's existing 6px of air.

**The repro is position-dependent and that is worth knowing**: it needs the boat's screen y inside a
narrow window (roughly 191–252 at 390×664), which is why the same pristine tree measured 65px of
overlap one run, 16px another and none a third. The controlled comparison zooms the camera all the
way out through the game's own `camFull`, so board rows map to fixed screen heights, and sweeps four
rows: rows 3 and 4 are byte-identical before and after (the clamp correctly does not bind), rows 5
and 6 go from 20px and 46px inside the card to 6px clear.

### F4 — the recipe cards stop scaling (index.html, one selector token)

The D-32 sweep's own exclusion list ends with *"the draft-pick recipe cards — the whole card is the
control, 300px of it. A scale pulse there is a large, slow wobble on the biggest thing on screen
rather than a 'tap me' cue."* **A recipe card IS an `.apBtn`** (`class="apBtn recipeCard"`), so the
selector caught the very surface the comment excused, and has since it was written. `:not(.recipeCard)`
is the rule the author already decided, finally said in CSS.

This is **not** an animation artifact of the screenshot, and the plan is right that solo-desktop-005 is
a settled state: the pulse is permanent, so the slicing is permanent too — just intermittent, which is
worse to read than a steady overlap. The focused card keeps its own orange ring pulse
(`pp4FocusPulse`, confirmed still running after the change) — the cue survives, the wobble goes.

**Rule 8 sweep, named:** every other `.apBtn` the sweep catches was checked. `.bkoCard` is a `<ul>`,
not an `.apBtn`, so it never had the pulse and needs no exclusion. The radial petals, the flat button
rows and the battle buttons keep it — they are small, which is the whole distinction the comment drew.

## Deviations from the plan

**1. [Rule 1 — bug, and the biggest thing in this plan] The stuck peek.** Not in the plan. Found while
measuring fault 5, in the same code path the fault named, and it is far worse than the fault: it makes
every prompt in the game invisible and untappable, permanently, with no way out a player would find.
Fixed and measured rather than logged, because a game that reads as frozen is not something to defer.

**2. [Reported, not fixed] Fault 5 does not reproduce as described**, and fault 6 does not reproduce
at all. Both are reported above with the measurements rather than "fixed". The gap rule and the
bubble's placement are correct and were left alone.

**3. [Rule 1 — my own bad reading, corrected in the open] My first pixel analysis of the gate PNG
concluded the bubble contributed no cream at all** — because I compared a point inside the bubble
against water 80px away with a different texture. The reading that stands crosses the bubble's own
top and bottom edges in one column. Recorded because the wrong version was one sentence away from
being filed as "the bubble is genuinely underneath".

**4. [Rule 1 — my own, caught by re-measuring] The first version of the F1 fix moved the fault.**
Padding the veil's foot alone put the ceremony title 5.9px into the wind ribbon. Caught by measuring

**5. [Process] The plan's diagnosis of fault 6 was a hypothesis, and it was wrong in a useful way.**

reaches the radial bloom, so the circles the judge measured were ordinary `.apBtn`. Answering the

**6. [Hygiene, not mine]** A headless Chrome from an earlier session was found running on port 9991
with a profile under this session's scratchpad, burning 11 minutes of CPU. Killed, scoped to that
port. Every probe this plan started was on its own port in 8471–8497 / 9471–9497 and is dead.
