# 02.2 — Parallel Paths Audit (READ-ONLY, D-29)

**Scope:** every place in `4/src` where two or more code paths must agree and nothing structurally
makes them. **Nothing was changed.** Wyatt decides what, if anything, gets done.

**Method note (rule 6):** every row below is marked CONFIRMED (the functions were read end to end,
and the code can only behave the way described) or CANDIDATE (the code difference is real, the
player-visible consequence is reasoned, not measured). **No browser was run.**

---

## 1. The answer to Wyatt's question — is item 4 one bug, or a third fork?

**It is one bug, and it is the visible corner of a fork — but not the fork you expected.** The bot
that pockets a dubloon for staring at the sea and *then* fires its ovens is real, and I read the
code that does it. But the split it comes from is not host-versus-guest. It is **the rules layer**:
there are three places that decide what a captain may do on their turn — the human's button menu,
the bot's on-screen turn, and the engine's headless turn — and only the human's menu was ever told
about the ovens. The commit that added the "Fire up the ovens!" button
(`90ce864`, *"give the ovens their button back"*) touched exactly one file and one menu; nothing
required the other two turn paths to learn the same rule, so they did not. **That is rule 13 and
rule 23 being the same disease in two layers, exactly as you suspected** — one is "two paths decide
what a captain may DO", the other is "two paths decide what a captain SEES", and both were built
the same way: a second consumer arrived and got its own copy instead of going through the first.
**The most serious thing this audit found is not item 4 at all.** It is that a guest in a crew game
**cannot bake** — when their ovens light, the bake-off bench opens on the *host's* screen and the
host answers it for them, because the bake-off prompt was deliberately written with no
remote path back when `/4` had no multiplayer, and multiplayer came back afterwards without anyone
revisiting it.

## 3. Per-finding detail

### F1 — A guest cannot bake. (CONFIRMED, and I would put this in front of him first.)

`4/src/ui/flow.js:565` and `:591` both say, in words, that there is deliberately **no remote branch**
in `bakeoffPrompt()`:

> *"THERE IS NO REMOTE PATH, and that is a decision rather than an omission… v2's welcome screen has
> no Host a Crew or Join a Crew (index.html), and in the two modes it does ship every human seat is
> local."*

That premise is **no longer true**. `4/index.html:1902` carries the comment *"Phase 2 (Multiplayer
Revival), D-08: 'Host a Crew' and 'Join a Crew' restored"*, and both cards are live. The bake-off is
on by default (`BAKEOFF_ENABLED = true`, `4/src/shared/index.js:391`).

So in a crew game: `runLiveDayBakeoff` → `bakeTurnLive` (`4/src/orchestrator.js:909`) calls
`bakeoffPrompt(p, …)` for **any** human seat, on the **host's** browser. `passGate()`
(`4/src/ui/lobby.js:276`) returns immediately outside Pass & Play, so nothing stops it. The bench,
the rewatch button and the shot clock all render on the host's screen; the guest gets one broadcast
line, *"X steps up to the ovens…"*. **The single most important moment in the voyage is played by
the wrong person.** The decision was sound when it was written; the ground moved under it.

*Cost L* because it needs the same local/remote seam `ask()` and `pickCell()` already have, applied
to a prompt with an animated bench and a purse that changes mid-decision.

### F2 — Item 4: three copies of "how a turn ends" (CONFIRMED)

- **Human:** `4/src/ui/flow.js:1795` computes `canOvens`, and `:1832` suppresses the Pass button when
  it is true (`if(!canOvens)opts.push({label:"🌊 Pass…"})`). So a human at Tortuga with a full
  recipe is offered *"Fire up the ovens!"* and **is not paid the pass dubloon**.
- **Bot on screen:** `botTurn()` ends at `4/src/ui/flow.js:2170` with a bare `g.doPass(p)` — no
  `canBake` test. `doPass` pays `cfg.passCoin` and emits the sea-creature line
  (`4/src/engine/index.js:958`). `runLiveDayBakeoff` then lights the ovens a beat later
  (`4/src/orchestrator.js:889`). Hence: **the bot passes AND fires the ovens, and is a dubloon richer
  than a human doing the same thing.**
- **Headless bot:** `Game.takeTurn()` does the identical thing at `4/src/engine/index.js:2742`, so
  the simulator and the browser agree with each other and both disagree with the human.

The graveyard is clean on this (rule 10): `90ce864` is the only commit on the ovens button, it
touched one file, and its message frames the change purely as a human-facing button. **Nobody
defended the bot's dubloon — nobody was asked.** Note also `botTurn`'s own comment at
`4/src/ui/flow.js:2158`: *"THE FALLBACK, and it has to be repeated HERE rather than inherited"* —
the file already knows it is a copy and says so.

*Cost S:* the shape already exists — `canBake(p)` is one shared predicate
(`4/src/engine/index.js:2765`), used by the human menu and by `lightOvens`. The question that is
**Wyatt's, not mine**, is whether arriving at the ovens should pay the pass dubloon at all; whichever
he picks, all three sites should read it from the same place.

### F3 / F4 — Storms and sails: the host watches, the guest is told (CONFIRMED)

`runStormLive()` (`4/src/ui/flow.js:1088`) is a director's cut: it pulls the camera wide before the
first hull moves, walks each ship one square at a time with a real pause between squares, and
narrates each outcome. The guest's `watchEvents()` (`4/src/orchestrator.js:1219`) receives the same
events and paints each ship where its snapshot says it is. Same for an ordinary sail:
`animateSailRoute()` is called from four places, all inside `humanTurn`/`botTurn`/`humanAct` — the
guest calls it from nowhere. **What is shared is the rim sweep**: `animateRimSweepIfAny()` is called
on both tiers, and the file says why. **That is the proof this is fixable and the proof nobody
finished the job** — one of three animations was made shared, two were not.

### F5 / F6 — There is no director on the guest at all (CONFIRMED)

`setActor()` is a one-line writer of `appState.curSeat` (`4/src/ui/util.js:1740`) and
`window.__pp4.actor(seat)` sets the stage's active seat. Between them they drive the top-bar boat
highlight (`4/src/ui/stage.js:405`), the camera's shot-hold, and the ask-pill anchoring. **All
eighteen call sites are on the host/solo path.** The guest's only camera signal is
`4/src/ui/stage.js:552-557`, which sniffs a captain's colour out of the narration HTML to guess who
the shot is about — a fallback doing the job of a system. This is the mechanism under two of D-26's
eight rows; it is recorded here to explain them, not to re-report them.

### F7 — Two different cadences for the same sound (CONFIRMED mechanism)

Host: `liveRender()` (`4/src/ui/panel.js:302`) takes `events[last]` and calls `spawnPops` +
`playForEvent` **once**, however many events the engine emitted since the last repaint. Guest:
`watchEvents()` runs the same two calls **per event**. Both lines even carry matching comments
("the host's per-event sound moment" / "the guest's mirror of it") — which is precisely the
discipline-not-structure signature rule 23 names. *I did not measure how often multiple events land
between two host repaints, so the audible impact is CANDIDATE.*

### F8 / F9 — The trade bot on screen is not the trade bot in the simulator (CONFIRMED code)

`botOpenTradeLive()` (`4/src/ui/flow.js:1990-2110`) is, by its own admission in the source, a third
copy of the settlement decision. It has been repaired once already (the counter-terms bug, commit
`69b9f23`) and two differences survive that repair: it asks **every** holder rather than the
`audience` the offer was priced for, and it prices a spare crate with a literal `1.1` instead of
`PLAN.leverageTurns`. The engine and the browser therefore run *nearly* the same bot. `settleTrade`
and `counterTerms` are properly shared — **the selection arithmetic around them is not.**

### F10 — Two ways to name a price (CONFIRMED)

`4/src/ui/util.js:1454`: `if(!decisionIsLocal(seat)) return coinStepper(...)`. The comment above it
predicted this exact situation and asked for it to be closed when `/4` shipped online multiplayer.
It has.

## 4. Deliberate divergences — **NOT** findings. Do not "fix" these.

- **The CAPTAINS panel row order.** *(This closes D-26's open question — it is deliberate.)* Both
  tiers call one function, `seatOrderFrom(appState.mySeat)` (`4/src/ui/util.js:76-86`), which lists
  seats in sailing order **rotated so the reader's own seat is on top**. Host and guest *should*
  show different first rows; that is the design. There is one builder and it cannot drift.
- **Who writes to Firebase, who creates the room, who runs the sim, who owns the shot clock.** All
  correctly gated on `appState.isHost`.
- **The bake-off's use of `botGuess` for every seat, human or bot** (`4/src/engine/index.js:2804`
  note) — deliberate, to keep the seeded stream from forking. Do not "optimise" it away.
- **`humanTrade` selecting no deal automatically** while the bot paths do — a human chooses, a bot
  is chosen for. Different by design, not a copy.
- **`localAsk` vs `watchPrompt` field set** — this *was* a drift class (seven fields, all found by
  eye) and it is now **gated**, see §5.
- **The host's blank recipe picker in screenshot 17b** — D-27, asked and answered.

## 5. What already enforces agreement — these are the models to copy

1. **`watchChat()`** (`4/src/orchestrator.js:1656`) — every client, host included, both sends and
   listens. Already named in D-25 as the shape for the one-director rewrite. It is the only place in
   the game where the host is a subscriber to its own broadcast.
2. **`optionButtonsHTML()`** (`4/src/ui/util.js`) — one button-row builder for the host, the guest
   and the draft channel, landed in `757ba1e`. It ended a seven-field drift class **by deletion**,
   which is what a real fix to this pattern looks like.
3. **`4/scripts/prompt_field_parity_check.js`** — a gate, aimed at `4/`, that fails **by name** when
   a prompt field crosses on one tier and not the other. This is the existing gate D-28 wants
   company for, and it is the proof the technique works here.
4. **`Game.ev()`'s per-event state snapshot** + `watchEvents`' mirroring block
   (`4/src/orchestrator.js:1256-1270`) — 02.1's fix. It deleted a lie rather than patching the six
   readers of it. The note in that block is worth re-reading before Group A starts.
5. **`Game.inPlay(p)`** (`4/src/engine/index.js:2764`) — one predicate replacing thirteen special
   cases for "is this captain still on the board". The elegant answer Wyatt asked for, already built.
6. **`doPass()`, `settleTrade()`, `counterTerms()`, `canBake()`, `sailHighlightRect()`,
   `animateRimSweepIfAny()`** — each is a single shared implementation, and each is the reason some
   neighbouring thing has *not* drifted.

**The pattern in all six: the fix is a deletion, not a synchroniser.**

## 6. Recommendation — if he fixes one thing

**Fix F1: give a guest their own bake-off.** Item 4 (F2) is the one he asked about and it is a
half-day at most — but it is a bot pocketing a coin and a line of narration reading oddly, and he can
still play around it. F1 is a captain who sails sixteen days, reaches the ovens, and **never gets to
bake** — the whole voyage's payoff handed to somebody else's browser, with no error and nothing on
screen to say so. It fails in exactly the way this project keeps getting hurt by: every check passes,
because the prompt genuinely rendered and genuinely resolved, just on the wrong machine. And unlike
the display forks (F3–F7), which Group A's one-director rewrite will sweep up on its own, **F1 sits
in the rules layer and no planned work touches it.** If it is not fixed deliberately, it will not be
fixed. F2 is small enough to ride along in the same sitting.

## AUDIT COMPLETE

- **10 findings. 8 CONFIRMED, 2 part-CONFIRMED.** F7 and F8 are confirmed as *code differences* read
  end to end, but their player-visible impact is reasoned, not measured — I am calling those
  CANDIDATE on impact and saying so rather than rounding up.
- **Item 4 is one bug AND the corner of a fork** — the fork is in the **rules** layer (three copies of
  "how a turn ends"), not the host/guest display layer. Rule 13 and rule 23 are the same disease in
  two layers, as suspected.
- **The biggest thing found was not on the list:** in a crew game a **guest cannot bake** — the
  bake-off prompt has no remote path (a decision made when `/4` had no multiplayer; multiplayer came
  back and nobody revisited it). Recommended as the one fix if only one happens.
- **D-26's open question is answered:** the CAPTAINS row-order difference is **deliberate** — one
  shared function rotates the list so the reader is on top. Not a fault.
- **F5/F6 explain two of D-26's eight rows** rather than adding to them: `setActor` and every camera
  call live only on the host path.
- **Nothing was changed. No browser was run. No process was started.**
