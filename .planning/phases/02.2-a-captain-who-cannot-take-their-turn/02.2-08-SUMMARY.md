# Phase 02.2 Plan 08 (Group F): the fan, his own five, and the phone leftovers Summary

**One-liner:** the crowded ring of action circles now has real air in it and drifts away from a
cornered boat instead of piling, Pass is always the lowest choice on screen, every coin flip in the
game takes the same second and a half, and the wind pill has stopped being run over by the
fast-forward button.

---

## What a player gets

**Tap a dock or open your action menu and the choices are separate, tappable circles** instead of
one packed slab — the gap between them went from about three pixels to about fifteen, and when a
boat is jammed in a corner with eight things to choose from, the whole cluster moves out into open
water rather than stacking on itself. **Pass is now always the bottom circle**, whichever way the
fan points, so it reads as the last thing to do rather than one option among the rest. **The italic
line that explains a greyed-out choice has stopped lying across the choices it is explaining.**

**Every coin flip now takes the same second and a half.** Before, a dock flip took a third of a
second and a battle flip took two-thirds — which is why they never felt alike.

**On a tablet or a desktop the wind and forecast are legible in the header row**, in front of the
dark gradient instead of behind it, and the fast-forward button can no longer be drawn on top of
them. **On a full desktop the menu has left the hamburger** and sits neatly under the captains box,
and **the turn-clock row is gone from it** — the chip in the header is the only place it lives now.

**And four of the five small phone faults are gone:** the "Docking at…" line no longer appears
twice, the "Play again!" button at the end of a voyage is on screen instead of two hundred pixels
below the fold, the fourth captain is no longer sliced in half by the bottom of the screen, and the
two recipe cards' ingredient lists line up even when one title wraps to three lines.

**Size:** all eight of the decisions this plan carries (D-44, D-46, D-47, D-48, D-49, D-50, D-51,
D-52), plus the two helper-line seams 260821-qwv measured and left. **It leaves undone:** the fifth
phone fault, which does not reproduce (below); the crew leg, which this plan never had; and two
pre-existing gate failures at one window size, also below.

## What changed, decision by decision

### D-44 — the fan stops packing, and when it cannot fit it drifts

**The gap was 6, and the circle was not 66.** `D` was the literal 66 copied out of the stylesheet;
a rendered petal is 68–70px, because `#pp4Prompt.radial .apBtn` carries a 2px border outside its
66px box. Every spacing in the placement function was therefore computed against a circle smaller
than the one on screen. `D` is now read off `menu[0].offsetWidth` — the renderer's own answer,
which is `BOARD-RENDERING.md` §7's rule one scale down — and the gap is a quarter of it. Four
petals plus three quarter-gaps is 331px, which still fits a 390px phone's band with room either
side; that is the measurement Wyatt was shown when he chose this.

**The drift is the new behaviour and it is what he asked for.** The search ladder was three fixed
steps (0, 14, 28) — a quarter of a circle's worth — after which the cornered grid packed everything
into a slab. It now steps outward in rings of half a circle-and-gap until the band's own diagonal is
exhausted, so the dock is reached only when nothing in the band holds the formation at the required
gap. And if even the dock piles, it says so with the numbers rather than handing back overlapping
circles in silence.

### D-44's two helper-line seams, both measured in 260821-qwv and both closed

**(a) The peek hint ran first and dodged last frame's layout.** `peekHintTick()` was called at the
top of the radial branch, before the pill, the helper line and every circle were re-placed. It now
runs at the very end of `tick()`, from one call site, after the whole placement pass.

**(b) `.apSub` was not an obstacle, and nothing lifted it.** It is in the fan's obstacle list now.
And when the cornered dock lifts the ask pill clear of the circles, the slider and the helper line
come with it — **lifting the pill's own height alone put its bottom 10px above the block and then
re-stacked a 23px helper line into that 10px**, which is the same fault reintroduced by its own fix.
Caught by re-measuring after the change, not by reading it.

### D-47 + D-52 — one fault, two costumes, fixed structurally

Design note 2 moved the wind pill visually into the header row and left it `position:fixed;
left:50%` — drawn in the row but not part of it. The ribbon's gradient washed over it (D-47), and
the ribbon's flex children, which cannot know it exists, walked into it (D-52: his Safari screenshot
reads `WI[⏩]NOW`). On desktop and tablet the pill is now a real child of the ribbon's flex row, so
spacing and paint order come free and the whole collision class goes with them. The phone keeps its
own fixed pill below the ribbon (D-18/D-31). The boundary is read through
`matchMedia("(min-width:601px)")` — the stylesheet's own line, not a second copy of 601.

**Rule 8 sweep, named:** `#pp4Fx` (21), `#pp4Cap` (22), `#pp4Prompt` (30), the peek fade
(`body.pp4Foot #pp4Pill{opacity:0}`) and the ceremony veil (44). The pill now inherits the ribbon's
own stacking (40 on stage) rather than competing with any of them, and the veil still covers it —
seen in the 820×1180 opening screenshot, where the pill is dimmed under the ceremony exactly as the
board is. The peek fade still works because it targets the id, not the position.

### D-52's other half, which he did not flag (rule 22)

The captains card was flush against the bottom of the window in the stacked branch **and 84px
taller than its own rows**. The mechanism, measured rather than guessed: `camFrame()` writes the
card's `top` inline every frame so it rises to meet the board, and the base rule pins `bottom:0` — a
`position:fixed` box with two explicit insets and `height:auto` stretches to fill, per the CSS spec.
(`measureCapNaturalHeight`'s own comment records this exact spec behaviour biting from the other
direction; it is the same trap, one branch over.) Desktop-stacked now releases `bottom` and is inset
by the same `--pp4CapGap` the side-by-side column is spaced by, and `computeStageGeometry()` reserves
that gap when it derives the board's size so the air can never be squeezed out.

### D-48 — Pass is always the lowest circle

`flow.js` already pushes Pass last into `opts`, which is why the flat card fallback has always been
right: DOM order puts the last option at the bottom. The fan maps index → spot and then picks the
most **open** heading, so fanning upward or sideways put Pass mid-cluster. **Stated the way the card
already behaves — the last option takes the lowest spot — and applied once**, to whichever set of
points the search produced, so it holds for the formation, the outward rings, the cornered dock and
the anchored-boats fan alike. A swap, not a re-sort.

**It needs no new field and nothing on the wire, so a guest gets it by construction.** An `acts`
array across the prompt payload was drafted and thrown away for that reason: a rule that has to be
carried to the other tier is a rule that can go missing on it, and this one cannot.

**One thing to know:** in the cornered grid a bottom ROW is what Pass joins, so with eight circles
it can share the bottom line with a crate rather than standing alone. Nothing is ever below it,
which is what he asked for, but "the lowest" is a row and not always a single circle.

### D-49 — every flip takes 1.5s, and the measurement corrects the diagnosis

**Two causes.** The dock flip slept a flat 340ms; a battle flip slept
`clamp(260, 650, stepDelay()*0.7)`, and `stepDelay()` is a flat 3000, so it slept **650**. Nearly
twice as long, same coin, same voyage — and that, not the latency, is the variation that measures.
The scheduling latency the CONTEXT diagnosis blamed is real but **small** in headless desktop
Chrome: tap→result ran 348–352ms against a 334–337ms paint→result, so about 15ms. On a loaded phone
it will be larger, and the fix removes it either way because the clock no longer starts where the
code happens to resume. **That correction matters and is recorded rather than glossed: the diagnosis
named the right mechanism and the wrong dominant term.**

The clock is stamped where the spin is **painted** — `setFlipCoin("spin")` in `board.js` is the one
spelling of a spinning coin in the game, reached by the dock tap, by `broadcastFlip` and by a
guest's Firebase listener alike — and each of the three call sites waits the remainder through its
**own** `sleep`, so fast-forward, pause and reload-replay behave exactly as before. A raw
`setTimeout` in `board.js` would have made a skipped battle crawl and a replay stall.

### D-50 / D-51 — the desktop menu, and the row that left it

**One list, two mounts, and the list is the node.** `#footerRow` is the game's only menu;
`buildStage` stamps it `pp4MenuList`, which is what carries the card look, the full-width rows and
the sound row, so the look belongs to the list rather than to either home. In side-by-side,
`computeStageGeometry` moves `#pp4Cap` **and** `#footerRow` into `#pp4Col` — a real flex column with
one derived gap — and the ☰ goes away with them. Everywhere else the card is its own fixed panel
and the menu is the ☰ sheet, unchanged. Moving the node is what makes "one list" true by
construction.

**A measured `top` for the menu was considered and rejected**: the captains card's height changes
whenever a row is opened, so anything keyed to a number refreshed on a timer would drift. A flex
column cannot.

D-51 travels with it: the turn-clock row and its CSS rule are both deleted, the toggle function
stays (the ribbon chip has always called it), and the sound row — which used to hand-copy its box
values from `#pp4ClockRow` — now owns them outright.

### D-46 — the five phone faults: three fixed, one fixed elsewhere in this plan, one not there

1. **The duplicated "Docking at…" line — FIXED.** One moment, two paths: `ask()` broadcasts the ask
   as a narration line for the deciding seat, and `localAsk` stashes the same words for the flip
   ceremony's title. A de-dupe existed but lived inside the radial branch and compared against
   `.apMsg` — and **a pure flip renders no panel at all**, so it could never fire for the one case
   that was actually duplicating. `retireEchoBubble()` now asks the question in one place, from
   `tick()`, for every prompt style.
2. **The clipped bottom toolbar over the End of Voyage card — FIXED.** It is the card's own "Play
   again!" button, and it is a **control**: D-43's accepted line (a scrollable card may be cut at
   the bottom) is about content, and D-38's ruling is that a control you cannot hit is the one
   unacceptable outcome. Sticky inside the scroll container.
3. **The sliced captain row — FIXED, and the cause was a percentage.** `camFrame()` reserved 30% of
   the window for the captains card; at 390×664 that is 199px and four rows are 231px. A percentage
   cannot be right for a table that has two, three or four captains and a hold that grows all
   voyage (rule 9). It now reserves what `measureCapNaturalHeight()` says the card needs, cached on
   the geometry clock, still ceilinged at 250px. **Cost, stated rather than hidden: the phone's
   board strip loses about 32px to the row it was cutting.**
4. **The stray "hov." text — DOES NOT REPRODUCE.** Walked every text node in the document with a
   TreeWalker and every attribute of every element, on the full board at 390×664 with all tiles
   drawn: "hov" appears in **no** text node and **no** attribute. `grep` over `4/src` and
   `4/index.html` finds it nowhere either. Reported as not reproducing, with the measurement, rather
   than "fixed" — a screenshot artifact or the judge misreading board art.
5. **The misaligned recipe rows — FIXED with subgrid.** A `min-height` on the title would be a typed
   number that is wrong for the first four-line recipe anybody writes. Subgrid is the version with
   no number in it: the two cards adopt the row track of the grid that already holds them, so the
   lists begin on the same line by construction. `@supports`-gated, so a browser without subgrid
   gets today's layout rather than a broken one.

## Rule 23 — where two things became one, and one place I chose not to converge

**Converged:** the flip clock (one paint stamp, three waits); the peek hint's placement (one call
site at the end of the tick); the echo de-dupe (one function, every prompt style); the menu (one
node, two mounts); the fan's gap (one derived value, five former numbers).

**NOT converged, deliberately:** the three `sleep`s that wait out the flip remainder are still three
calls in three files. That is the seam that keeps fast-forward, pause and reload-replay working —
`orchestrator.js`'s `sleep` and `flow.js`'s `sleep` clamp to 40ms under ⏩ and resolve instantly
during a replay, and `board.js` cannot know about either. **The clock is shared; the transport is
not, and should not be.**

## Deviations from the plan

**1. [Rule 1 — bug] The plan's D-44 key link says `NEED = D + 6` is "the fan's separation pass". It
is only one of five.** `NEED = D + 6` governs the anchored-boats branch alone; the ordinary fan
spaces at `D + 8`, the cornered dock at `D + 6` and `clash()` at `D + 4`. Fixing only the named one
would have left the crowded case — the cornered dock, which is what an eight-button phone fan
actually hits — untouched.

**2. [Rule 1 — bug] `D = 66` was wrong by 4px.** The plan and CONTEXT both take 66 from the
stylesheet. A rendered petal is 68–70. This is why the "6px gap" measured 2.8px.

**3. [Rule 1 — bug, mine] My own fix reintroduced the fault it fixed.** Re-stacking the helper line
under the lifted ask pill put it back on top of the circles, because the lift cleared the pill's own
height and not the stack hanging off it. Caught by re-measuring after the change, not by reading it.

**4. [Rule 2 — missing critical functionality] The gate had never measured the stacked branch.**
`stage_layout_check.mjs` sampled five sizes that between them hit only phone and side-by-side. Added
820×1180 and a branch-coverage check that refuses to call a full run complete while a whole shape
went unlooked-at. Coverage, not a per-bug assertion (D-37).

**5. [Rule 2, instrument] The gate was photographing its own 404, and then could not photograph
anything.** The contact sheet's URL was built relative to `REPO` and fetched from the run's own
server, so any `--out` outside the repo produced `../../../tmp/...` and python answered 404 — the
exact class `d9c9a71` hardened `playtest_gate.mjs` against, unfixed here, and the second time this
project has paid for it. Serving the sheet from its own directory fixed that; the capture then
**timed out on a six-size run at 40s, 90s and 240s alike**, while a one-size run finished in
seconds. So that step has never once succeeded on a real multi-size run — it only appeared to,
because a three-line error page photographs instantly. **The screenshot step is deleted.** The HTML
sheet is kept (it links the real full-resolution PNGs, now clickable, with every failure beside its
tile) and the check is now "does every PNG this sheet names exist on disk with real bytes in it",
asked of the filesystem, which an error page cannot answer.

**6. [Rule 3 — blocking] The probe lost its first two runs to a helper that vanished silently.**
`window.__gate` was defined once at the top and then the page was reloaded, so every click reported
"not clickable" and the real error (`__gate is not defined`) was swallowed by the caller's own
`return null`. Written into the file.

**7. [Reported, not fixed] The name modal arrives pre-filled**, so a probe that types into it
captains a boat called "Davy SconesDavy Scones" — which shows up in every screenshot as a clipped
captain name and reads exactly like a layout defect. Fixed in the probe (triple-click first, as the
layout gate already does). Not a game bug; recorded because it will fool the next instrument too.

## What the gate says, and the two failures that are not mine

Final run: six sizes, all three branches, 64s for the slowest, 18 of 18 screenshots on disk, zero
console errors. `RESULT: FAIL`, on two checks, both at **820×1180's OPENING moment only**:

```
FAIL stacked: captains card sits below the board
FAIL captains card does not cover the board
```

**Both reproduce identically on the pre-fix build** — the same run on build `h` before any of this
plan's changes reported those two plus `captains card hugs its content — no empty tower (card 315px
vs content 204px)`. **Three became two; the one this plan owns is fixed.**

**What the remaining two actually are, measured.** `m.board` is `#boardwrap` — the *camera strip*,
not the painted square. At the opening the board is at full zoom and the square **letterboxes**
(`preserveAspectRatio: xMidYMin meet`, `BOARD-RENDERING.md` §4), so the strip runs to y=956 while
the painted board ends at y=865, and the captains card correctly rises to meet the *picture* at 865.
At the sail and action-menu moments the camera has zoomed in, the square fills the strip, and both
checks pass. **The card is not covering any board art — read in the screenshot
(`shots/f-12-gate-820x1180-opening-after.png`), the 90px it overlaps is empty void below the
letterboxed square.**

So this is the gate comparing against the wrong box at full zoom, which is `BOARD-RENDERING.md`
§7's own lesson ("if your check needs a formula, the formula is the thing most likely to be wrong").
**I did not change the assertion.** Weakening a check to make a run green is how a gate stops
meaning anything, and I have not measured this enough to be sure the strip is the wrong box in
every case. **Left for whoever owns the camera work, with the measurement above.**
