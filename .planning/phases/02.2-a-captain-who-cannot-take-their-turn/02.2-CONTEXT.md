### The architecture, found by comparing his screenshots — 2026-08-20, second pass

> **How this was found matters, and is the reason rule 22 now exists.** The decisions above
> (D-05…D-07) were reached by *reading code*. They are not wrong, but they answered a question Wyatt
> did not ask. He then said: *"look at the screenshots from my PDF and see how the host screenshots
> look different than the guest screenshots… compare them pixel by pixel."* **Doing that took
> minutes and produced the finding below, which reframes the whole of Group A.**

- **D-24: There are TWO DIRECTORS, and that is the root cause of every host/guest divergence.**
  Measured at `4/src/orchestrator.js:1654-1655`:

  ```js
  if(appState.isHost){ runLiveNet().catch(…); }
  else{ watchEvents(); watchPrompt(); watchNarr(); watchFlip(); watchBattle();
        watchDraftPrompt(); watchClock(); watchTurnOrder(); watchRecoveryState(); }
  ```

  **The host's screen is driven by the game loop** — `runLiveNet()` plays the voyage and calls the
  renderers directly as it goes (e.g. `showAhoyIntro()` at `:977`). **The guest's screen is driven
  by nine independent Firebase listeners**, each deciding on its own what to draw and when.

  **They share the renderers** — `showNarration`, `localAsk`, `setActor`, `flash` — which is exactly
  why 02.1's state-layer fix helped and why nobody spotted this. **What is NOT shared is the
  orchestration: who calls those renderers, in what order, and when they are cleared.** Indicative
  measure (line-count proxy, not exact call sites): `showNarration` appears 2× in `ui/flow.js` and
  7× in `orchestrator.js`; `localAsk` 7 and 13; `setActor` 13 and 7; `flash(` 14 and 9. **Every
  renderer has two independent sets of callers that can drift, and have.**

  **THIS is the "two parallel code paths" Wyatt named in item 18.** Not `isHost` conditionals —
  two orchestrations. D-06 named two narration call sites; that was one symptom of this, correctly
  traced but far too narrow. **D-06 and D-07 are superseded by D-25, not withdrawn:** the narration
  divergence is real and is one instance of the general fault.

- **D-25: Group A is rewritten around ONE DIRECTOR. Wyatt's pick, 2026-08-20: "do it properly."**
  The host becomes **a listener to its own broadcast** — it computes the game and publishes events,
  then draws from that same stream exactly as every guest does. One director. A change to how
  anything is drawn then reaches both screens *because there is only one place it is drawn from* —
  drift becomes structurally impossible rather than something kept in sync by discipline.

  **The precedent already exists in the same file, one line below the fork:**
  ```js
  watchChat(); // unlike narr/ev, every client (including the host) both sends and listens for chat
  ```
  **Chat already works the way he is asking for.** It cannot drift. The game display never got it.
  **Use `watchChat` as the shape to copy — it is a worked example, not a theory.**

  He was told plainly that this is bigger and riskier than the patch, and that it touches the host
  path which mostly works for him today. He chose it anyway. **D-04 still applies: if it balloons,
  land what is safe, write down exactly what is left, and move to Group B.**

- **D-26: THE EIGHT DIVERGENCES — his evidence, and Group A's real acceptance list.** Read off his
  side-by-side screenshots. Group A is done when a two-tab session reproduces **none** of these:

  | His shot | Host shows | Guest shows |
  |---|---|---|
  | 17a — Ahoy | the message once, in the centre card | **the same message twice** — a dark top strip *and* the centre card |
  | 17b — the wait | "⚓ Waiting for yer mateys…" | "⚓ Everyone's choosing their recipe…" |
  | 17c — drawing lots | the text once | **the text twice** — a bubble behind, plus the card |
  | 19 — waiting | **no narration at all**, board reads as dead | prompt still up |
  | 20 — the director | camera parked elsewhere | camera centred on the active boat |
  | 20 — sail window | **no sail squares** | sail squares highlighted |
  | 20 — CAPTAINS panel | Wyargh's row first | his own row first — **different order** |
  | 21 — top-bar boats | updating with the turn | not updating |

  **The CAPTAINS row-order difference is OBSERVED, NOT YET EXPLAINED** — it may be deliberate
  ("me first") rather than a fault. Measure before treating it as a bug (rule 6).

- **D-27: The host's recipe picker in 17b is NOT a fault. Asked and answered.** It appears blank
  beside the guest's populated one; Wyatt: *"the picker works, i just screenshotted it before the
  text finished animating in."* **Do not add it as an item and do not "fix" it.** Recorded because
  it looked exactly like a ninth divergence and a later session comparing the same image will

### Preventing the recurrence — his two rulings, 2026-08-20

> Wyatt, on being shown the two-directors finding: *"this is terrible engineering… it violates my
> design principles, and it should never have been built. How can you ensure that you don't engineer
> things like this again?"* The answer became **CLAUDE.md rule 23 (ONE DISPLAY PATH)** plus the two
> decisions below. **A written rule is the weakest of the four preventers and is recorded as such** —
> the gate that fails, and two screens side by side, are the ones to trust.

- **D-28: The host/guest parity gate is pointed at `4/` NOW, inside Group A — written RED first.**
  His pick over waiting for Phase 3. **`scripts/host_guest_parity_check.js` already exists and was
  written for exactly this failure** — its own header: *"the set of prompt classes the HOST path
  emits (`localAsk`, `src/ui/flow.js`) equals the set the GUEST path emits (`watchPrompt`'s ask
  branch, `src/orchestrator.js`). Fails NAMING the class present on one side and missing on the
  other."* **It reads `REAL_ROOT/src/` — the OLD game — and there is no `4/` copy.** It has been
  green throughout, on a game nobody is developing. *A gate aimed at the wrong tree is not silent,
  it is reassuring* (`docs/HARD-WON-LESSONS.md` §3).

  **This is re-aiming an existing script, not new tooling** — rule 7 is satisfied. **It must be
  proven to FAIL against today's two-directors tree before the Group A rewrite turns it green**;
  otherwise it proves nothing, and this project has had three checks in one night measure something
  other than what they named. **Without it, Group A is verified by screenshots alone and nothing
  stops the next change silently re-forking the path.**

- **D-29: A read-only AUDIT of the same pattern elsewhere — report, change nothing.** His pick:
  *"audit and report, fix nothing."* Find every place where **two or more code paths must agree and
  nothing structurally makes them**, and bring him a list with what each would cost. **He decides
  what is worth doing and when. This is not licence to start fixing.**

  **The lead that prompted it is his own item 4.** There are at least **four** turn implementations:
  `humanTurn()` (`4/src/ui/flow.js:1919`), `botTurn()` (`:2113`), the engine's headless `takeTurn()`
  (`4/src/engine/index.js:2742`), and the guest's listener path. Research found the bot passes AND
  fires the ovens because **neither bot path checks `canBake(p)` before `doPass(p)` — a gate the
  human menu does have.** **Marked a strong lead, NOT a measurement** — `humanTurn` and `botTurn`
  have not been read end to end (rule 6).

  **If it holds, rule 13 (bots and humans have identical rules) and rule 23 (one display path) are
  the same disease in two layers** — the rules layer and the display layer — and item 4 is not one
  bug but the visible corner of a third fork. **That is the question the audit answers.**
