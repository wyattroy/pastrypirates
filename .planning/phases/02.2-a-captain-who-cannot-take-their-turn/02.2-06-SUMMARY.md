## Item 6 — the Bake-Off card (D-16)

### What a player gets

**Before:** you finish your bake, the card scores you — and then it just stays there. Measured on a
real solo voyage: **twenty-five seconds after the guess resolved the card was still on screen**,
spent, its button greyed to "In the oven…", still reading "2 of 5 in place". Behind it, **DAY 2 was
running**: three narration lines stacked unreadably in the corner, the board dimmed, holds changing
under a card nobody could dismiss. Then attempt 2's bench rendered into the same box. The card was
continuously up across two whole days of play.

**After:** you get a reading beat on the verdict — sized by how long the sentence actually is — and
then **the card goes**. You are back on the board, with the camera following whoever is sailing and a
readable narration bubble over their boat.

`shots/d-06-item6-BEFORE-card-still-up-on-day2.png` · `…-BEFORE-straight-into-attempt2.png` ·
`…-AFTER-verdict-readable.png` · `…-AFTER-card-gone-board-back.png`

### The finding that made it small

**It is a consistency fault, and the bake-off was the odd one out.** Every other centre-stage card in
this game already ends itself with the same two statements:

| Card | Where | How it ends |
|---|---|---|
| `localAsk`'s `done()` | `4/src/ui/flow.js:221` | `delete pp4Stage; panel("")` |
| `dryCeremony`'s button | `4/src/ui/panel.js:1219` | `delete pp4Stage; panel("")` |
| `passGate`'s hand-off `took()` | `4/src/ui/lobby.js:307` | `delete pp4Stage; delete pp4Hand; panel("")` |
| **the bake-off** | `4/src/ui/bakeoff.js` | **nothing — this was the bug** |

**Surfaces swept for gesture consistency (rule 8), as the plan asks be named:** those three
centre-stage cards above (all four now end identically); hold-the-sea, which fades every floating box
but deliberately **not** the centre-stage intros or the flip-ceremony veil — Wyatt's own sanctioned
exception, left exactly alone; and the shot-clock forfeit teardown (`activePickCleanup`), deliberately
**not** given the new exit, because the reveal that follows a forfeit needs the bench still in the DOM
to animate the verdict onto.

`retireBakeCard()` is named by all three of the card's exits — the finished reveal, a reveal with
nothing to reveal onto, and a malformed bench. **No new state:** reaching the end of the reveal *is*
the attempt being spent, since the reveal only ever runs after `bakeResolve` has scored.

**The old comment was wrong, and it is worth knowing why:** it called the stage-flag delete "belt
only" because "the stage itself ends when the narration that follows replaces the `.bko` content".
Measured — that replacement never happens. Narration in this game draws into its own bubble, not into
`#actionPanel`. Nothing downstream was ever going to take this card down.

**One consequence handled rather than shipped:** retiring the card makes the verdict hold the entire
reading window, where before a slow reader could simply keep looking. A flat 1300ms would give a
70-character verdict the same beat as a 39-character one (rule 9), so it now reads
`narrationHoldMs()` — the one reading-speed model the game paces every line by, which Group E landed
hours earlier (D-34/D-45). `VERDICT_MS` survives as the floor, so nothing that shipped got shorter.

## Item 16 — a joining captain keeps the name they typed (D-19)

### What a player gets

**Type a name a real person at the table already has** → a red line appears **under the name box**:
*"Arrgh — a captain aboard already sails as Bess. Pick another name, matey."* No popup, no frozen
tab. Your typed name stays in the box, you edit it, Join works.

**Type a name one of the bot captains has** → **you get it**, straight in, and the bot quietly takes a
different name. Nobody at the table shares a name.

**"Change yer name" in the lobby** → same rule, same words, same place.

Before tonight there was **no collision check of any kind** for a typed name — it was written to the
seat verbatim. Only the blank-name fallback ever checked anything.

### Both collision paths, exercised in two browsers

The plan is explicit that neither is visible in a screenshot of a normal join, so both were driven in
**two real Chromes against a real Firebase room** (`name_collision_2browser.mjs`, 23 assertions,
lobby only, room deleted afterwards):

| | Result |
|---|---|
| **Human collision refused** | Warning shown under the box with the right words, red border on the box, the guest gets **no seat**, the host's lobby is unchanged (no second Bess). `shots/d-16-guest-refused-human-name.png` |
| **Not a frozen tab** | The liveness probe returned. A blocking `alert()` suspends the page's JS, so a CDP evaluate against it never comes back — reaching the next line *is* the proof, without needing to detect a dialog. |
| **Bot collision granted** | Guest typed "Dough Hook", got it; seat 2's bot moved to "Davy Scones". **Both screens agree seat for seat**, no duplicates on either. `shots/d-16-guest-granted-bot-name.png`, `shots/d-16-host-sees-bot-swapped.png` |
| **Red-proofed** | The warning is asserted **absent** before the colliding join, so "it appeared" cannot be one that was already there. |

### Three write paths, not two

The plan named `joinRoom`'s fresh claim and `renameMySeat`. There is a **third**: `joinRoom`'s
**rejoin** path (NAME-01/C), which exists precisely so a captain can back out and come back under a
different name — i.e. the path *most* likely to collide and the easiest to forget. One rule,
`applyNameClaim()`, named by all three; a gate asserts all three call it.

**The check runs inside the Firebase transaction**, so the check and the write are one atomic step.
Checked before it, two captains typing the same name in the same instant both pass and the last write
wins — the bug wearing a fix's clothes (T-02.2-21).

**An unnamed bot seat still holds a name**, and the whole thing turns on this: `createRoom` writes bot
seats as `{name:"", id:"", bot:true}`, so nothing in the database says "Crustbeard" — yet `pname()`
draws the seat-indexed default, so that *is* the name at the table. `seatHeldName()` is now the one
answer, read by the collision check and by `unusedDefaultName`'s taken-set alike.

**Its one behavioural change, listed rather than hoped:** a bot seat carrying a *written* name now
counts as holding that name rather than its index default — which is exactly what item 16 creates.
Every other reachable input is unchanged, and the one divergent case (a claimed seat with a blank
name) is unreachable, because no write path in this codebase ever produces one. **Verified directly:**
solo and pass-and-play roster naming across six cases — no duplicates, no blanks, and
`unusedDefaultName(null,0)` still returns "Davy Scones".

**Security dispositions from the plan's threat register, all discharged:** the warning is written with
`textContent`, never `innerHTML` (T-02.2-22 — the string is a player-authored name every other client
also renders); it is inline DOM, never `alert()` (T-02.2-23); the claim is transactional (T-02.2-21).

## Deviations from the plan

### [Rule 1 — the plan's own check was measuring the wrong thing] Task 2's inline grep, replaced

The plan's `<verify>` asserted that the word `bot` appears inside `joinRoom`'s body. That was a fair
proxy while the rule was going to be written *inline* — but the rule is written **once** and named by
three callers, so the word moved and the grep went red on correct code. **The only way to satisfy it
would have been to write the rule out three times**, which is the fault being fixed.

Replaced with `4/scripts/name_claim_check.js`: **43 assertions that import and RUN the rule**, plus a
wiring section asserting all three callers reach it, no `name: chosen` write is left anywhere, the
refusal uses `setNameWarning` and not `alert`, and both warning mounts exist. **It red-proofs itself**
by re-running its two load-bearing assertions against the pre-D-19 behaviour and requiring them to
catch it — a check only ever seen passing is indistinguishable from one that cannot fail.

To make that possible, `applyNameClaim` and `withoutSeat` moved to `4/src/shared/index.js`, the pure
leaf tier, where a Node gate can import them with nothing stubbed. They belong there anyway, beside
the two helpers they are built from.

### [Rule 2 — a consequence of my own change] The verdict hold is now derived

Covered above. Retiring the bake-off card turns `VERDICT_MS` into the whole reading window, so leaving
it a constant would have shipped a "you cannot read the verdict" fault created by the fix itself.

### [Rule 1 — my own instrument was wrong] A D-21 sighting that was a battle card

**Reported here in full because it nearly became a phantom defect, and that is the failure this phase
was created by.** The crew leg's D-21 watching brief fired: 4 empty prompts on the host, 10 on the
guest. It looked like a sighting.

**It was a battle card, rendering correctly.** My predicate listed the things it expected a prompt to
contain — `.apMsg`, `.apBtn`, `.btlBtn`, `.sailCell`, the armed coin, `.bko` — and called the panel
empty when it found none. A battle card between its phases carries none of those six.

Established with a **control**, not an argument: the same predicate run in **solo**, where D-21 cannot
exist by construction (no guest, no room). It fired there too, and the captured detail named it —
800–935 characters of `<div class="btl">`, painted, opacity 1, visible.

This is the exact class that produced three phantom defects at the 02.1 gate ("checking for buttons on
a prompt kind that draws squares on the board"). The predicate now asks the **renderer** whether
`#apGridInner` has any painted element child — nothing to enumerate, and a card it has never been
shown cannot be mistaken for an empty box. **Re-run: 0 on both sides. D-21 did NOT reproduce**, and
that is now a measurement rather than a shrug.

### [Not a deviation — a doc updated in the same commit as the change that made it true]

`4/src/ui/index.js` carried the invariant *"bakeoff.js reaches no further than panel.js/state/shared"*.
Item 6 adds `util.js` (for `narrationHoldMs`), so that line was updated in the same commit. It widens
nothing in practice: `panel.js`, which `bakeoff.js` already imports, imports `util.js` itself.
