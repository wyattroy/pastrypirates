# Phase 02.15 Plan 01: One Log, One Display Path — Summary

**Six of Wyatt's seven divergences are closed and shipped as build `2026-08-20b`. The seventh — the
host's missing sail squares — was deliberately not attempted, and reverts through one conditional
that is named below.**

The host no longer draws narration or the active seat from a path a guest cannot reach. Where there
were two orchestrations there are now two shared entry points — `flash()` for a narration line,
`applyActiveSeat()` for whose turn it is — and both tiers go through them. **The host still never
reads its own screen back through Firebase**, which is what keeps the single-player game alive.

### Why Stage 4 was not attempted, and how to do it later

It buys **one** of the seven shots at the price of touching the path every player uses to take every
turn, in solo, pass-and-play and networked play. Its failure mode is not cosmetic — it is *a captain
who cannot take their turn*, the fault this phase is named after. Stages 1–3 landed green, so the
gate was open; the cost/benefit was not worth it in the same session, and **D-04 is Wyatt's own
ruling that stopping here is a success.**

**The seam is one line.** `4/src/ui/util.js`, in `ask()`:

```js
const base=decisionIsLocal(seat)?netHandlers().onLocalAsk(...)
                                :netHandlers().onRemotePrompt(...);
```

Stage 4 moves that fork so the host draws its own prompt from the local dispatch. **It must NOT be
made to round-trip through Firebase** — `remotePrompt` has no `db` guard and is unreachable in solo
only *because* of this fork, and `decisionIsLocal` returns true for every human seat at a
pass-and-play table. That is written up in the director map.

## Item 18 — the plain-English answer Wyatt is owed

He asked: *"You were going to redesign the architecture of multiplayer so that guests and hosts are
all shown with parity, but not from two parallel code paths… Did this get done?"*

**Partly, and here is the honest shape of it.**

The two parallel paths were never the `isHost` branches anyone kept blaming. They were **two
directors.** Your screen, as host, was driven by the game itself as it played. A guest's screen was
driven by nine separate listeners, each deciding on its own what to draw and when. They shared the
drawing code, which is why earlier fixes helped — but nothing made them agree about *when*.

**What changed:** for the narration and for whose turn it is, there is now **one place**, and your
screen goes through it exactly as a guest's does. That is why six of the seven things you
photographed are gone. **What did not change:** when the game asks *you* a question, your screen
still draws that question the old way. That is the missing sail squares, and it is the one on the
list still standing.
