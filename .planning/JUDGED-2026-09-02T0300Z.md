# WHAT THE PASS FOUND

## 1. THE LAST SCREEN OF THE GAME, AGAIN — a second mode, the current build, and now SCOPED

**`solo-phone-023-settled.png`.** The automatic judge found this on its own, unprompted:
*"award cards' text … clipped/overlapped by the fixed 'Play again!' button sitting on top of them."*

Opened by eye, it is the identical fault the 02:19Z watch found on `passplay-phone-039` — down to
the detail. *The Silver-Tongued Ledger*'s winner, **Davy Scones in pink**, is sliced horizontally:
only the tops of the letters clear the button's frosted backing. *Crustbeard* on the right card
survives because it sits a few pixels higher.

**What is new, and it matters twice over:**

- **It is not one screen in one mode.** That was pass-and-play; this is **solo**. It is what the End
  of Voyage does on a phone.
- **IT IS PHONE-ONLY, AND THE TABLET IS A WORKING REFERENCE.**
  `solo-tablet-031-settled.png` — same run, same build, same screen — shows **four** award cards in
  one row with **every winner's name fully legible** (Davy Scones, Crustbeard, Dough Hook, Flaky
  Jack), the whole stats table readable, and *Play again!* sitting clear **below** the content.
  Nothing is covered.

**So the posed pair rule 26 asks for already has its third picture.** Whoever fixes this poses
390×664 before and after and checks the result against the tablet, which is what the screen is
supposed to look like. Chart row updated rather than duplicated.

## 2. THE "BAKE THIS!" PILL COVERS THE PASTRY YOU ARE ABOUT TO CHOOSE — AND IT IS DELIBERATE

**This is filed as a design question for Wyatt, NOT as a defect, and the correction is the point.**

**The prediction, written before anything was opened:** the openings have never been judged, the
three faults already found are all the same shape (something on top covering text or art), so expect
at least one more of that shape — most likely on a phone. **What would prove it wrong:** the phone
legs' 001–015 come back clean.

**What was seen.** On the recipe picker, the highlighted card's orange *Bake this!* pill sits on top
of that recipe's pastry art and covers nearly all of it, while the unselected card beside it shows
its pastry in full. Verified across **six legs, all three modes, three sizes, both engines** —
`solo-phone-003`, `passplay-phone-003`, `solo-tablet-003`, `solo-tablet-wk-003`,
`crew-phone-guest-003`, `passplay-desktop-003`. The starkest is `crew-phone-guest-003`: *Mexican
Chocolate Torte* is a blank cream sliver, *Molten Chocolate Lava Cake* beside it is fully drawn.

**THE PREDICTION WAS WRONG, AND THE GRAVEYARD IS WHY.** Commit `4d1bc6cd` (2026-08-13) says it in
plain words: *"A 'Bake this!' pill now appears **over the pastry art** of the tapped card."* It was
built to solve a different problem — *"nothing said how to commit a recipe; the confirming second tap
was undiscoverable."* And it has not drifted: `.pp4Bake { top:35px }` is unchanged since that commit
(`git log -S`), and the stage's `.recipeThumb { height:54px }` since `511c4270`, 2026-08-11 — two
days **before** the pill existed. **The pill has always sat exactly where it sits.**

`docs/INTENDED-BEHAVIOUR.md:113` covers the neighbouring false positive (*"one card highlighted and
one not is the two-tap selection state"*) but not this. Checking it was rule 28 working, and reading
the commit log was rule 10 working — without both, this would have been filed as a bug and would
have been the fourth false positive in that family.

**Why it still goes to Wyatt.** He asked tonight (INBOX-20260902T0048Z) *"what is the maximum size
they are displayed at?"* about this very art, while deciding whether to compress it. The honest
answer has a part nobody had said: **on the one screen where a player compares two pastries to
choose between them, the selected one is not really displayed at all.** That is a taste call, it is
his, and it is now in BLOCKED ON WYATT with the trade stated (moving the pill may put back the
discoverability problem it was built for) and a recommendation marked.

## 3. ONE JUDGE FINDING ASSESSED AND NOT PASSED ON AS A DEFECT
