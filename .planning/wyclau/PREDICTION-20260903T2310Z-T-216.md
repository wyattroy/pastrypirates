## What I expect, and WHY

**I expect 2 to 4 of A–K to be wrong or materially misleading, and I expect the wrong ones to be
the sharp edge-case clauses rather than the headline sentences.**

The why is not a hunch about any one rule — it is where this page came from. Every word is the
in-game modal, which is prose a human wrote to describe the game **at the time they wrote it**, and
the engine has moved since (the sanctuary ruling is exactly that: a 2026-08-06 engine change that
the modal never caught up with, and whose own code comment four lines above still states the old
rule). A headline sentence — *sailing is free*, *a crate costs 6 minus what's left* — gets re-read
by everyone and stays true. **A qualifier bolted onto the end of a sentence is what nobody re-reads.**

So my three named suspects, in order:

1. **A's "Across the wind doesn't count against ye."** The cheapest implementation of an upwind cap
   is to test whether a step has any component against the wind, which would make a pure crosswind
   step *not* trigger the cap but any diagonal that is half-across and half-into it *would* — and
   the page's flat "doesn't count" invites a player to plan a route the game refuses.
2. **I's "Land and other ships stop ye short."** Two different stoppers named in one clause, and I
   would bet on land being implemented and ships being either ignored or handled differently. This
   is the same shape as the sanctuary sentence: a confident "that's the whole of it" attached to a
   list.
3. **G's "Nobody's paid on a battle that ends with no winner."** The break-off and the slip-away are
   two distinct no-winner endings and a bounty payout has to handle both.

**I expect F's core ladder (heads beats tails, downwind wins a double-heads, crosswind is a
collision) to be RIGHT** — it is the most-played path in the game and a divergence there would have
been found by a player long ago.

- **If the wrong ones turn out to be headline sentences rather than qualifiers**, my whole reasoning
  about *where* drift lives is wrong, even if the count happens to land in range. The count being
  right for the wrong reason is not a hit.
- **If a claim I call wrong turns out to be `docs/INTENDED-BEHAVIOUR.md` material** — the game
  working as designed and the page describing it loosely — then I have made rule 28's mistake and
  the finding is mine, not the page's.

## THE TRAP, NAMED

**I want to find wrong claims.** A watch that audits eleven sentences and reports "all fine" looks
from the outside exactly like a watch that did nothing, and I know it. That bias pushes in one
direction: toward reading an ambiguous or compressed wording as a defect. **A rule stated loosely
for a player is not a false rule.** The bar I am holding myself to is the one CEO 181 set on the
sanctuary sentence: a claim is FALSE only when the game does something a player acting on the page
would be surprised by — and I must be able to show it by RUNNING the code, not by reading it. The
sanctuary comment four lines from the sanctuary code stated the old rule in full; reading is how
that error survives.
