## THE EVIDENCE WAS PERISHING WHILE THIS WAS BEING WRITTEN, AND HERE IS THE RATE

Every trial writes its screenshots to the **same filenames** in `sea-trial-shots/`, and writes
`judge-queue.json` **last, once, to the same path** (`scripts/playtest_gate.mjs:673-682`). Nothing
stamps a picture with the run that took it. So the moment a second trial sails, the first run's
queue is still on disk pointing at pictures that now belong to the second run.

**145 screens lost in fifteen minutes**, to the trial this machine has running. Waiting made it
worse, not better — which is the opposite of what the first version of this file concluded.

**PRESERVED, AND THAT IS THE FIRST THING THIS WATCH SHOULD HAVE DONE:** `judge-1914Z-shots/` now
holds the 1914Z run's own `judge-queue.json`, its `runid.json`, and every screenshot of its that had
not yet been rewritten — **221 of the 343 queued screens; 122 were already gone.** Gitignored by the
`*-shots/` glob, and meant to be deleted once judged.

`node scripts/qa/judge_the_queue.mjs --snapshot=<dir>` is that step, and
`--judge=<dir>` is the judging pass: it writes `judge-results.json` **after every batch**, skips
anything already judged, and **drops a screen whose picture did not survive rather than judging
whatever now sits at its old path.** Any number of sessions can share one pass.

**What a player sees:** in a trade, the button telling you *whose* offer you are about to accept is

circle's compact label as `short:` — the captain's name as line one of a two-line label inside a
fixed-diameter disc, with nothing sizing the name to the circle. Every captain whose name is longer
than "Walk away" is a candidate.
