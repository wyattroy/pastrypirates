Analytics — the whole picture

Written for Wyatt, 2026-09-03. Every number here was read off the live database this morning, not remembered. Your ask: “give me instructions to switch it on, and give me the full plan for analytics as an artifact.”

The short version

You already have a Google Analytics property, and it has never been told anything. Its ID is sitting in the game's Firebase settings. Switching it on is one line of HTML per page — about ten minutes of work.

You also already have your own analytics, and it is better than you think. It has been quietly recording since 10 August. That is where the numbers below come from.

So the real question is not “should I add Google”. It is “which three or four things does Google know that my own counter doesn't?” There are exactly three, and they are listed further down. Everything else Google would tell you, you are already collecting.

1. What you have right now, without Google

The game writes three tiny anonymous records to your own Firebase database — a page boot, a voyage start, and a voyage finish. No names, no third party, nothing that follows anyone off your site. It is in src/ui/usage.js, and your /stats.html page reads it back.

⚠ Do not read “123 people, only 19 ever played”

That sentence is roughly what the raw numbers say, and it is unfair to the game in four separate ways — a previous note quoted it flat and it was wrong to:

  • Crew guests never count as a start. Only the person who hosts a crew game gets counted. Six crew games were hosted, so their guests all look like people who opened the page and walked away.
  • Coming back to a saved voyage counts as nothing. Resumes and replays write no start at all — the file says so in its own header.
  • Private tabs mint a new browser every time. So the 123 is inflated, and testing inflates it most.
  • The “unfinished” count is deliberately generous. A voyage finished after a mid-game refresh still counts as finished, but loses its link back to its start.

All four biases push the same direction. The real drop-off is better than 123 → 19, and nobody yet knows by how much. Google would not fix this either — it would have exactly the same blind spot about crew guests, because the blind spot is in when the game decides to count, not in who is counting.

3. What Google gives you that you don't have

Honestly: three things, and one of them you could build yourself in an afternoon.

WhatWorth it?
Where people came from — the referrer. Whether your 123 browsers arrived from a Google search, a link someone shared, or by typing the address. The strongest reason. You are about to launch; this is the one that tells you whether anything you do to promote it works.
Roughly where in the world, and on what — country, device type, browser. Useful. You have been guessing at the phone/desktop split; this ends the guess.
Every page, for free, forever — including About and Rules, and any page you add later, with no code to remember to wire up. This is the quiet one. Your own counter needs a line added per page; Google's needs nothing after the first install.

The honest caveat: the referrer is not something only Google can see. Any page can read its own referrer in one line of JavaScript, and your own counter could store it beside the build string it already stores. So “I need Google for the referrer” is not true — “Google gives me the referrer plus geography plus every page, today, for ten minutes of work” is true.

4. The two decisions that are yours

Decision 1 — which pages get the tag?

  1. The public pages only — the game, About, and Rules. Recommended
    This is the smallest thing that closes the real gap. It leaves your private console out, so measuring yourself doesn't pollute the numbers, and leaves /classic out because it is frozen and already has its own counter.
  2. Every page including /classic — one consistent picture, at the cost of mixing a frozen v1's traffic into your launch numbers.
  3. The game page only — the most cautious, and it leaves About and Rules exactly as blind as they are today, which is the thing this was meant to fix.

Decision 2 — a cookie notice, or not?

This is a genuine judgement call and nobody here has made it for you. Plainly:

  • Google Analytics 4 sets a cookie and sends data to Google. Your own counter does neither.
  • Children play this game, which is the part that makes this more than a formality.
  • There is a middle option: GA4 has a setting that stops it storing anything on the visitor's device — no cookie, and no attempt to recognise a returning visitor. You lose “how many unique people” from Google, but you already have that number from your own counter, which is why this option fits your situation unusually well.
    ⚠ This is the one thing on this page I could not check. The machine that wrote it has no web access, so this rests on how the setting has worked rather than on Google's documentation as it stands today. It gets verified before anything is installed — and if it has changed, decision 2 becomes a straight choice between a banner and no banner.
  1. Cookieless mode, no banner. Recommended
    You keep the three things you actually want (referrer, geography, per-page), you set no cookie, and no child is asked to consent to anything. Your own counter keeps supplying the unique-player count.
  2. Standard GA4 plus a small consent banner. More complete data, and a box over your front door on every first visit.
  3. Standard GA4, no banner. What most small sites do. Simplest, and the one with the most to argue about.

5. How to switch it on — the instructions you asked for

Step 1 is yours and takes about ten seconds. The rest is mine. Nothing has been installed. This page is the plan; say which options you want and the next watch does it.

  1. Confirm the property exists. Open analytics.google.com while signed in as you, and look for a property whose measurement ID is G-2KK6EZDZSP.
    That ID is written into the game's Firebase settings in two files. Google normally only writes that line when Analytics is switched on for the project — so the property almost certainly exists, but I cannot see inside your account and I am not going to claim it as verified. If it is not there, making one is three clicks and produces a new G- ID to use instead.
  2. Tell me your two answers — which pages, and cookie or cookieless. Both are above with a recommendation marked.
  3. I add one snippet to the top of each chosen page. It looks like this — about eight lines, and nothing else in the game changes: 
    <script async src="https://www.googletagmanager.com/gtag/js?id=G-2KK6EZDZSP"></script>
<script>
  window.dataLayer = window.dataLayer || [];
  function gtag(){dataLayer.push(arguments);}
  gtag('js', new Date());
  gtag('config', 'G-2KK6EZDZSP');     // + the no-storage setting, if you pick cookieless
</script>
    ⚠ Use the snippet Google's own setup screen prints you, not this one. Step 1 puts you on that screen anyway, and it is the authoritative version — this is here so you can see the shape and the size of the change, not so anyone copies it. Same reason as the caveat under decision 2: this machine could not reach Google's documentation to check the current form.
  4. I flip the check that is currently red. There is already a check in the repo that fails today precisely because the ID is declared and no page loads it. It gets renamed and wired into the test suite, so that from then on deleting the tag by accident fails the build.
  5. You look at the live site and see data arrive — Google's “Realtime” view should show your own visit within a minute or so. That is the proof it worked; nothing else is.
    Same caveat as decision 2: this machine could not reach Google's docs, so treat the timing as “quickly, watch for it” rather than a number to hold them to.

One thing that must not be skipped

This touches index.html — the page real players are in the middle of — with a third-party script. The repo's rules say a change to that file gets the deepest level of testing before it goes live: ten voyages across three screen sizes and both browser engines. Today's trial took 75 minutes (read off this morning's report, not estimated; the rules themselves budget about 85).

You already spotted this and said so: “it clearly doesn't need a full one given that you're just adding a tag to index; so we need a way to tell sea trial that and manually choose the depth of the trial.” You are right, and that is now its own job on the Chart — a separate watch, because it changes the testing machinery rather than the game. It is not folded in here.

6. What this plan does NOT do

  • It installs nothing. You asked for instructions and a plan; that is what this is. No page has been touched.
  • It does not decide consent for you, and no session should.
  • It does not build the sea-trial depth control — your third sentence. That is filed as its own item.
  • It does not verify your Google account. Only you can open that.
